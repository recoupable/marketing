## Where they agree

### 1. Own is not the job. Operate is the job.

Sachin Saggar (RedBrick), ep 01 `[38:32]`:

> "you've got to first of all, know what you own and understand what levers you have to pull."

Henry Marsden (Fix Music), ep 02 `[36:43]–[36:52]`: data-room quality
"will directly affect what value you can offer and knowing what value you
can then create once you've made that transaction."

Evan Bogart (Seeker), ep 11 `[15:10]–[15:32]`: peers "buy a bunch of rights
and then try to figure out what to do with it afterwards." Seeker inverts
that — strategy before close, including "something we can do right away
when we acquire this, that's going to change the trajectory."

Cliff Fluet (Lewis Silkin), ep 15 `[05:53]`:

> "you're not buying royalty statements you're buying underlying rights"

**Recoup translation:** a catalog without an operating system is a term
sheet. The product is the levers.

### 2. Capital got ahead of infrastructure.

Saggar, ep 01 `[22:21]`: "a lot of capital that come into the sector, but a
lot of the services and infrastructure around that sector" lagged. He
compares it to renewables, which spent a decade building the stack
(`[22:56]`).

Marsden, ep 02 `[39:35]–[39:54]`: big funds are waking up but have "legacy
business and operations and infrastructure to navigate." New entrants have
a clean slate "to bake that in now."

Tom Mullen (SI), Catalog Maturity Curve Fastball, ep 08 `[01:55]`: as the
market matures, "operational maturity becomes a more important driver of
investor" returns than the original buying frenzy.

Larry Miller / Tom, ep 27: majors won by building **shared distribution
infrastructure** (`[10:35]`, `[14:40]`). Specialists win in the gaps the
platforms leave (Tom's A24 frame, `[26:04]–[27:21]`).

### 3. Data quality is a multiple, not a chore.

Michael Poster (counsel), ep 03 `[10:04]`: well-prepared catalogs "are able
to command a higher multiple versus those who don't even at that low level."

Marsden, ep 02 `[40:54]`: "a direct line, like a mathematical line between
catalogue cleanup, clean metadata, clean publishing registrations and
ownership picture that's disseminated everywhere" and future upside.

Alaister Moughan (valuation), ep 10 `[01:56]`: if you want "ongoing
valuations and hold yourselves accountable to the underwriting case through
the life cycle of a fund, you're really going to need a different tool set"
than a point-in-time model.

Golnar Khosrowshahi (Reservoir), ep 07 `[15:22]`: they must manage data
"not just for data that's coming in for catalogs that we own, but also when
we're doing this underwriting."

Firdaus Dawood, ep 18 `[01:43]–[01:50]`: cost of capital is a "confidence
tax" — opaque cash flows raise the return a lender requires. Music is often
"**unrated at close**" so the lender underwrites on an internal risk rating
(`[05:57]`).

### 4. The edge is increasingly *which rights* and *which machine*, not "music."

Tom Tyler (Rezonate): producer/mixer royalties — cash-flowing, operationally
hard, therefore underpriced (ep 04, opening thesis + `[23:32]` bottlenecks).

Cutting Edge (ep 14 `[07:26]`): film/TV / media music — the picture being
watched is the trigger, not a standalone stream.

Primary Wave (Adam Lowenberg, ep 06): reactivation / brand / seasonal
optimization of icons, not a DCF debate.

Pophouse (ep 24): artist **brand** as the asset, catalog as the foundation;
they still "underwrite cash flows bottom up, some by song" (`[07:06]–[07:32]`).

Benji Rogers (ep 05): AI licensing design will reprice catalogs that were
underwritten as if the next decade equals the last.

Richard Hinkley (ep 19 `[07:14]`): leakage as **surface area** — is the
recording actually on the DSP in that market, at contemporary standards.

### 5. Creative upside should sit *on top of* underwriting, not inside it.

Bogart, ep 11 `[09:07]–[09:26]`: "we're not underwriting any of what we can
do creatively." Ciaran Mulligan (M&G) calls that "marrying the financial
discipline and the creative discipline."

Camille Jamet, ep 26 `[04:18]–[06:07]`: pick tracks by save rate and
tracks-per-listener, not the current biggest streamer.

That is the institutional-friendly version of Moneyball: buy the cash
flows you can defend; treat A&R/sync/social as alpha, not as the case.

### 6. Decay is a fact, not a spreadsheet identity.

Alex Bowler, ep 22 `[00:00]`: "decay is inherently uncertain" even with all
the data. Saggar: it varies by genre, artist, and even song (`[02:09]–[02:15]`);
streaming growth used to **mask** decay (`[04:55]`). Herzfeld, ep 17
`[04:41]–[04:48]`: seven years as the street convention for "mature," with
genre/territory caveats (`[10:18]`).

## Where they split

| Axis | Camp A | Camp B | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vehicle** | Permanent capital / public co / "company not a fund" (Reservoir, Round Hill's evergreen argument, Duetti) | Closed-end fund with a known exit | Golnar ep 07 `[08:22]–[08:46]`; Gruss ep 20 `[12:56]`; Tibon ep 28 `[03:37]` |
| **What you buy** | Hits / cultural icons (Primary Wave, Pophouse) | Overlooked layers: producer royalties, film/TV, B-sides, emerging, young masters | Lowenberg ep 06 vs Tyler ep 04 vs Cutting Edge ep 14 vs Duetti ep 28 `[15:05]` |
| **How you win after close** | Reactivation / brand / NIL (Primary Wave, Pophouse, Seeker frontline A&R, Jamet streaming) | Rights-admin / leakage / registration (Fix Music, Repped, Hart) | ep 06, 11, 24, 26 vs ep 02, 19, 25 |
| **Time horizon** | "We're not managing for an exit" | 5–10 year fund (Gruss: catalogs don't fit) | ep 07 `[08:22]`; ep 20 `[12:56]` |
| **Velocity** | Few large, hit-dense deals | Industrialized volume (Duetti "over 80 deals every month" `[38:20]`; Fluet: playbooks or you destroy value-per-deal) | ep 28 vs ep 16 `[02:25]` |
| **AI** | New royalty engine if licensed with attribution (Rogers) | Not yet in the comps | Rogers ep 05 vs Saggar ep 01 `[25:31]`: "real tangible elements in data? Probably very limited" |
| **Who the LP is** | Public shareholders, family offices, insurers (M&G Life), ABS (Apollo / Concord) | Risk-on equity buying younger catalogs at lower multiples | Richards ep 09 `[08:26]`; Mulligan ep 11; Sipio ep 23 |

Golnar's split is the cleanest sentence in the corpus, ep 07 `[08:29]`:

> "we're not managing the way a private equity investor would manage or the way a fund structure would be managed in that it has a known exit at a certain time."

Gruss, ep 20 `[12:56]`: "it doesn't match a five to 10 year timeframe that way."

Tibon, ep 28 `[03:37]`: "you have the equity that sits behind the platform and then you have the debt, which sits behind your acquisition engine."

If Recoup is advising a catalog owner, the first question is **which of
those vehicles they are**. The operating system is different.

## SI's own operating ladder (Catalog Maturity Curve)

Host-only Fastball, ep 08. Four stages, four pillars. Quote Tom, don't
paraphrase into a fake fifth stage.

**Stages** (ep 08 `[03:42]–[05:33]`):

1. **Manual** — thorough but "reliant on heavily fragmented data."
2. **Operational** — visibility improving, some automation.
3. **Automated** — dedicated data engineers, often an in-house platform;
   reporting to external investors gets easier.
4. **Proactive** — "using the data to proactively identify risks or
   opportunities for future investments." Aspirational.

**Pillars they benchmark** (ep 08 `[07:36]–[08:46]`): data & infrastructure ·
legal · financial (underwriting vs ongoing valuation) · operational
(metadata, sync, UGC).

**Move-up sequence they preach** (ep 08 `[09:57]–[11:08]`): (1) **fix the
leaks** so "any investment into future growth would not fall through the
cracks," (2) **increase revenue** on existing assets (sync, brand plays),
(3) **future catalog acquisitions** with "data sits at the very center"
(`[12:13]`).

**Not everyone should be at stage 4** (ep 08 `[14:06]`). Match the curve to
strategy and investment cycle.

Ep 12 is the data-infrastructure *project* version of the same idea: honest
benchmark of where you are (`[01:51]`), stack-rank where you want to go
(`[05:33]–[06:05]`), then a plan from A to B (`[01:28]–[01:36]`). Don't
conflate it with ep 08's leak → growth → acquire ladder.

Recoup overlap: this is the same ladder as "skills/OS before Heartbeats
integrations" — don't automate a leak.
