# How to host a music-catalog podcast

> Recoup playbook, mined from Standard Innovation's Music Moneyball.
> All 28 episodes transcribed (2026-09-10). Moneyball skeleton verified on
> eps 01–06 and re-checked on later hours (walk-on + "on repeat" / quick-fire
> close still in place through Duetti ep 28). Fastball / live / crossover
> rituals are in §5–§5b.
>
> Corpus: [podcast archive](README.md). Cite `raw/transcripts/<slug>.txt`
> + timestamp. ASR names are untrusted — identity from RSS credits.

This is the show Recoup would be copying if it launched a catalog podcast:
three hosts who actually work in the space, a guest who runs a specific
machine, and a conversation that teaches the listener how that machine works.

---

## 1. What the show is (so the agent doesn't invent a format)

Two products, one feed:

| Format | Runtime | Job | Who's on mic |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Music Moneyball** | ~40–75 min | One operator, one thesis, enough time to get into the plumbing | Emma + Tom + Sam (usually all three) |
| **Music Fastball** | ~15–30 min | One mechanism (maturity curve, deal process, leakage) | Often Tom or Emma alone with a specialist |
| **Live** | ~40 min | Community recording; panel, not interview | Tom + two specialists |
| **Crossover** | ~54 min | Co-hosted with another show (Musonomics) | Tom + the other host |

Positioning line, identical cold-open every episode:

> "This is Music Moneyball, the podcast that explores how to unlock the hidden value in music catalogues."
> — ep 01 `[00:00]`, repeated ep 02 `[00:00]`, 05 `[00:00]`, 06 `[00:00]`

Sponsor line, also identical:

> "Brought to you by Standard Innovation, the data consultancy for investment funds."
> — ep 01 `[00:09]`

They never hide who pays for the show. The consultancy *is* the point of view.
If Recoup hosts one, name Recoup the same way: a point of view, not a nameless
"brought to you by our sponsors" bumper.

## 2. The host bench (roles, not titles)

Observed across eps 01–06:

| Host | What they actually do on mic |
|---|---|
| **Emma Griffiths** | Opens, reads the prepared bio, runs the icebreaker, lands the close, keeps energy human. Production/host. |
| **Tom Mullen** | The operator-host. Asks the infrastructure / process / "how does that actually work" questions. Picks up when the conversation needs a model, a bottleneck, or a comparison to another asset class. |
| **Sam Morey** | The commercial/market host. Asks "what's your angle," "have you seen anyone do this with data," supply/demand, multiples. Slightly more buyer-framed. |

Recoup equivalent: don't host this as a solo founder talking-head. Pair a
warm interviewer (Emma's job) with someone who can interrogate a decay curve
or a rights-admin stack (Tom's job). A third mic is optional; two is the
minimum that makes the conversation feel like a working session instead of
a Q&A.

They talk to each other, not just to the guest. Emma will tee Tom: "do you
want to wrap up then?" (ep 04 `[37:50]`). Tom will hold a thought ("Hold that
thought" — ep 05 `[02:20]`) and return to it. That is directing, not
interrupting.

## 3. Prep (this is the actual edge)

The guests arrive **already holding answers**. That is not an accident.

### 3.1 The walk-on song is homework

Emma, ep 01 `[01:08]–[01:15]`:

> "if we could bring you on with a song and if we had an actual budget for music licensing for our podcast and could play the song as you arrive, what song would that be?"

Sachin, same episode `[01:20]`:

> "This was such a tough question. I was scratching my head all day yesterday trying to think."

Henry, ep 02 `[01:52]`: "I was thinking about this." Benji, ep 05 `[01:39]`:
"So I thought about this." Adam, ep 06 `[01:54]`: treats it as a real
question, not a bit.

**Playbook:** send the walk-on-song question 24 hours out. It does three jobs:

1. Proves you asked something *about them*, not just their fund.
2. Gives the open a story instead of a résumé dump.
3. Hands the hosts a callback for the close ("if you could own one song").

Emma even jokes they'll insert it "when we do get the budget" (ep 01 `[01:53]`).
The constraint is named; they don't pretend they have sync.

### 3.2 The bio is researched, specific, and read cold

Not "today's guest is a music entrepreneur." Concrete facts, in order:

- Career arc (session bass → Bespoke → ATM → Fix Music) — ep 02 `[00:23]–[01:12]`
- Named catalogs / stunts (Aerosmith lottery scratch cards, Alice Cooper hot
  sauces, Whitney Houston slots, Smokey Robinson holiday) — ep 06 `[00:31]–[00:51]`
- Prior companies (PledgeMusic, Verify, Lark42, Sureel) — ep 05 `[00:40]–[00:57]`
- The *angle of this episode* ("today, in honor of the holidays, we're going
  to focus on … Bing Crosby") — ep 06 `[01:00]–[01:16]`

Emma on Crosby, ep 06 `[01:28]`:

> "As I said, I've researched Bing Crosby's entire backstory."

**Playbook:** the host who opens should be able to name 4–6 facts the guest
would recognize as *their* story. If you can't, you're not ready to record.
The bio also **scopes the episode** — Adam is CMO of a whole firm; they
tell the listener this hour is the Crosby holiday playbook, not a tour of
Primary Wave.

### 3.3 They've read the guest's writing

Ep 05 `[01:21]`:

> "we've been geeking out about all your fascinating writings and opinions for weeks now."

That is how Benji's episode can open on AI licensing instead of "so tell us
about your background." The background already happened in the bio. The
first real question is the thesis.

## 4. The episode arc (Moneyball)

Stable across eps 01–06. Recoup should steal this skeleton.

```
0:00  Cold open (tagline + sponsor) — 15s
0:15  Hosts + prepared bio + "please welcome X"
1:20  Walk-on song (pre-asked)
2:30  First real question = the machine, not the résumé
      "what do you do at RedBrick"
      "what's actually tangible in AI progress"
      then: market → thesis → plumbing → risks
~35:00  Close ritual (see §6)
~38:00  Where to find you + subscribe bumper
```

**Do not** spend the first ten minutes on origin-story fluff. The bio did
that. Origin is allowed *when it explains the machine* (Henry's keyboard
player → why he became a publisher, ep 02 `[01:54]–[02:51]`).

### 4.1 First real questions (steal these shapes)

Job-to-be-done first, not "how did you get into music":

| Shape | Example | Source |
|---|---|---|
| What is the firm | "give us an overview about what you do" | ep 01 `[02:10]` |
| What changed | "over the past five years, there's been a lot of capital coming into the sector. So what's changed" | ep 01 `[03:37]` |
| What's the angle | "when somebody is coming into the marketplace, the real question is, what is your angle? What is your approach? What's different about what you're doing?" | ep 03 `[05:58]–[06:16]` |
| Is the hype real | "is there anything that you're seeing that's actually tangible in terms of the progress that we're making?" | ep 05 `[02:45]` |
| Scope this hour | Crosby holiday playbook, not all of Primary Wave | ep 06 `[01:00]` |

### 4.2 How they guide (not a questionnaire)

- **Callback.** Benji's walk-on song is "20 Million Things to Do"; Tom
  immediately: "Can we do them with AI? Hold that thought." (ep 05 `[02:07]–[02:21]`).
  The icebreaker becomes a motif.
- **Name the tension, then ask both sides.** Tom, ep 04 `[06:39]–[06:52]`:
  "typical art meets science… how fractious do you find that relationship?
  …how do you bring them back together?"
- **Ask for the bottleneck, not the vision.** Ep 04 `[22:18]–[23:32]`:
  "are we actually serving the lower end of the market?" then "what are the
  primary bottlenecks… deal sourcing, due diligence, rights management."
- **Force a buy-side / sell-side stance.** Ep 03 `[12:03]`: "are you typically
  buy side or sell side or mixture of the two?"
- **Don't let "data" stay abstract.** Follow with "have you seen anybody
  taking a really novel approach to using data and technology to do that?"
  (ep 03 `[06:50]`).
- **Let the guest teach a checklist.** Henry on 350 registration places
  (ep 02 `[16:08]`) — they stay with the unsexy number until it's a picture.
- **Co-host as editor.** One host runs warmth; one runs mechanism. They
  don't both ask the same question.

### 4.3 What they do *not* do

- They don't pitch Standard Innovation mid-interview. The bumper already did.
- They don't fact-check the guest in a "gotcha" way. They pressure-test
  *the model* ("are we serving the grassroots?").
- They don't pretend they have a music budget. They name the constraint.
- They don't skip the human close. Every Moneyball hour ends as a person,
  not a term sheet.

## 5. Fastball is a different show

Don't run a 20-minute episode on the Moneyball skeleton. Verified across
eps 08, 10, 12, 15–16, 18, 19, 21, 26:

- **Open:** "Hello and welcome to Music Fastball" (ep 08 `[00:00]`, 12 `[00:00]`,
  18 `[00:00]`). Not the Moneyball tagline. Sam can host (ep 21 `[00:00]`).
- **One mechanism:** maturity curve, deal process, leakage, cost of capital,
  streaming upside. Host-only is allowed (eps 08, 12).
- **One specialist, often one host.** Tom or Sam; Emma is optional.
- **No walk-on song.** No long bio. The first minute *is* the thesis.
- **Close = wrap the mechanism**, not the six-question human ritual
  (ep 08 `[15:23]`; ep 16 `[15:00]` "before we wrap up one final question";
  ep 19 `[25:10]`). Subscribe bumper still lands.

Recoup should use Fastball for *teach a tool*, Moneyball for *teach a firm*.

### 5b. Live and crossover

**Live** (ep 22): cold-open is a **clip of the thesis**, not the tagline
(`[00:00]`). Tom + two specialists + "a live audience" (`[00:51]`). Panel,
not interview — they teach a concept (decay) to a room that already works
in rights. Recoup equivalent: record at a conference, pick one contested
model, two people who disagree on calibration.

**Crossover** (ep 27, Musonomics): two hosts as equals, a **market-history
frame** ("who wins after the catalog gold rush"), then an analogy from
another industry (Tom's A24: majors serve the middle of the bell curve,
specialists take the underserved edges — `[26:04]–[27:21]`). Recoup
equivalent: co-host with a show that already owns the listener, bring the
operating-system POV, don't re-interview a fund.

## 6. The close ritual (Moneyball)

Nearly identical outro. Recoup should keep it — it's how a finance show
stays a music show.

1. **Gig / cultural memory** — "is there one or two that really stand out"
   (ep 04 `[33:38]`).
2. **Pinch-me moment** — "what's been your biggest kind of pinch me moment"
   (ep 01 `[37:49]`, ep 04 `[32:39]`).
3. **Underrated opportunity** — "the most underrated opportunity in music
   rights at the moment" (ep 01 `[38:25]`, ep 04 `[35:03]`).
4. **One song you'd own, and why** — explicitly "you're going to get the
   royalties" (ep 01 `[39:05]–[39:13]`, ep 04 `[35:50]`).
5. **Where to find you** — website, email (ep 01 `[40:03]`).
6. **Subscribe bumper** — "subscribe at standard-innovation.com… Spotify,
   Apple, or YouTube" (ep 01 `[40:19]–[40:31]`).

The underrated-opportunity question is the **content mine**. Sachin's answer
is A&R / artist development (ep 01 `[38:32]–[38:53]`), not a multiple. That's
the clip.

Later Moneyball hours keep the ritual even when they rename it: Herzfeld
ep 17 `[43:07]` "quick fire round"; Duetti ep 28 `[47:10]` still starts
with the song "on repeat." Steal the *shape*, not the exact five prompts.

## 7. Question bank (catalog-podcast, not generic interview)

Steal the *shape*. Rewrite in Recoup's voice.

**Machine**

- What do you actually buy — masters, publishing, producer/mixer, brand/NIL, or a company?
- Walk us through one deal you would do again, and one you wouldn't.
- What's the bottleneck that keeps you from deploying faster: sourcing, diligence, rights admin, or capital?

**Market**

- What's changed in the last five years that is *not* interest rates?
- Who pays the premium, and why can they?
- Is this market short of catalogs or short of operators?

**Data / ops**

- If I handed you a messy data room tonight, what's the first leak you look for?
- Where does metadata turn into dollars (not "hygiene")?
- What would a well-prepared catalog command vs. the same songs, unprepared?

**Angle**

- What's your angle that a major / a PE fund / a family office cannot copy?
- Are you a fund or a company, and what does that let you spend on?

**Risk**

- What trip hazard do buyers still underestimate (reversions, escalations, AI licensing, registration gaps)?
- If streaming is flat, where is the next basis point?

**Human (close)**

- Walk-on song (sent the day before).
- Pinch-me. Underrated opportunity. One song you'd own.

## 8. Topics this feed already covers (don't repeat blindly)

See `guests/_index.md`. The map is: valuation (Saggar, Moughan, Herzfeld) ·
ops/data (Marsden, Hinkley, SI Fastballs) · deal law (Poster, Fluet, Katz) ·
overlooked rights (Tyler / Rezonate, Cutting Edge) · AI (Rogers) ·
reactivation/brand (Lowenberg / Primary Wave, Pophouse) · institutional
capital (Richards / Artisan, Sipio / Apollo, Dawood, Mulligan / M&G) ·
fund structure (Gruss / Round Hill, Tibon / Duetti) · creator-side
(Hart, Jamet, Hamilton).

A Recoup show should **not** re-interview the same thesis. Pick the gap:
e.g. the operator who runs the catalog *after* the IC memo, the rights
admin who actually registers, the A&R who moves streams.
