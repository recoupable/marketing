# Account research and relevant follow-up

Prepared 2026-09-14 from the company research and consulting's audience methods.

## Start with companies

Read the [research guide](../research/music-rights/README.md). Use the evidence CSV as the working source and the priority file as a starting subset. A company name in a directory or a podcast is a discovery lead; verify what it actually owns or operates from a primary source.

For each account, keep its name, website, segment, source URL, supporting quote, date checked, review status, relationship to other companies, and a specific reason to investigate. Separate a verified business description from an inferred operational problem.

Review parents, acquisitions, shared founders, and operating brands before counting unique buyers or creating CRM records. Use a stable account identifier in the engine; display names and domains can change. Existing customer, active opportunity, and no-contact exclusions should come from private CRM records when that connection is authorized.

## Then find the relevant people

Find the person who owns the work and the executive who can fund a change. Verify current roles with the company site or another reliable primary source. Keep the source and date. The selected public executive list is a starting point, not full coverage of every account.

Do not infer personal emails. Use the team's approved sources and private storage for contact data, and respect contact preferences. The public company CSVs are not a contact-upload list.

## Draft a reason to talk

Read the actual source, announcement, post, or prior conversation before responding. Explain why this particular observation is relevant to this particular person. Offer a useful insight or a specific question about the work; do not pretend to know an internal problem from public data.

Keep the draft linked to its account evidence and source observation. Follow the [discovery method](discovery-and-proposals.md) if it becomes a conversation. Do not call a response a qualified opportunity until the buyer's problem, owner, and constraints are understood.

## Keep paid distribution separate

The prepared company files support a company-first LinkedIn audience. The full account research is broader than the priority core. Matching, executive filters, exclusions, live reach, creative, objective, budget, and campaign dates still need to be configured and reviewed in Campaign Manager. Recheck current platform requirements before that work.

Company records do not tell us how many members will match. The user approved the research and its public repository home; this does not authorize ad spend or message sending. Stage the exact audience and creative for the relevant approval when the team is ready.

## Record what happened

Store outreach and replies in the approved private system, linked to the account, source, draft version, and owner. Show the difference between prepared, approved, sent, replied, meeting held, qualified, proposed, won, and lost. Only advance a state when the corresponding event occurred. Handle declines and duplicates before any next action.
