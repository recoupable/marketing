## Business lessons to keep in view

**A researched market is not an obtainable market.** The company file contains employer and operating-brand records, not a count of independent purchasers. Any revenue forecast also needs assumptions about sales cycles, conversion, customer retention, delivery capacity, and price. Advertising match counts and pipeline outcomes are still missing. The private forecast is not a public revenue claim.

**Recurring invoices and reusable software are different.** A services retainer can be valuable, but if each account requires substantial founder work, growth still depends on delivery capacity. Track implementation, support, and software usage separately. Measure the time and direct costs needed to serve each customer in private operating records.

**Build a repeatable customer result.** As consulting reveals repeated work, capture its inputs, steps, checks, output, and exceptions. The opportunity is to deliver the same useful result for another team with less custom effort. A generic chat interface or an isolated API call does not prove that result.

**Connect to tools customers already depend on.** The discussion considered catalog, royalty, audio-library, and file-storage systems. A promising starting point is improving work across existing tools. Replacing an established system of record would require much stronger evidence of a switching benefit. No vendor price or replacement plan is established by this document.

**Test usage frequency and importance.** A candidate paid workflow should solve something the company repeatedly needs, produce a result general chat tools do not reliably provide on their own, and matter enough that someone owns its success. Daily use was a desired trait, not a verified usage pattern or fixed pricing rule.

## Experiments worth exploring with engineering

| Experiment | What to try | Evidence needed before selling it as a product |
|---|---|---|
| Reliable statement preparation | Take representative royalty statements, convert them to a target import format, reconcile totals, and show exceptions for review | Accuracy against known outputs, handling of unfamiliar formats, time saved, and an owner who will pay for the result |
| A persistent workspace for recurring work | Save approved context, source files, completed work, and reusable procedures so the next run starts from a known state | Teams returning to use it, successful handoff to another operator, and clear access boundaries |
| Catalog activity and outcomes | Record which action was taken on which catalog or track, then compare observed outcomes over time | Reliable activity records and outcome data; correlations must not be sold as proof of causation |
| Reusable services behind the API | Extract a repeated, validated operation into a tool that can serve more than one customer | Repeat use, measured costs, reliability, and willingness to pay separate from consulting |

For the first experiment, a parser returning “success” is insufficient. The output must reconcile to the source, preserve identifiers, expose uncertainty, and be accepted by the downstream workflow. Use synthetic or approved private examples; no customer statements belong in this public repository.

For workspace experiments, local files do not automatically mean data stays on the device. Trace which model or service receives each input and honor the customer's approved access arrangements.

These experiments are product discovery alongside consulting. They are not the GTM engine's first implementation task. The engine should help us find the right conversations, record evidence, test messaging, and learn which recurring problem deserves investment.
