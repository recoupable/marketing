# Recoup team workflow worksheet

Use one copy for each recurring job. Ask people to bring a recent example they are allowed to share. Keep completed copies in your team's own workspace.

## Before inviting the team

Name the project owner, who will read responses, how long responses will be kept, and whether names are optional. Explain the purpose in plain language. Do not promise anonymity if the collection tool records identities. Collect process descriptions rather than confidential artist, contract, or financial records.

## Start with a recent piece of work

1. What did someone ask you to deliver last week? What started the request, and who needed the result?
2. Describe the finished output. How did the recipient decide it was usable?
3. Walk through that instance from the first request to acceptance. Which steps involved waiting, copying information, interpreting it, checking it, or correcting it?
4. How many times did this happen in the last complete month? Label counted work separately from an estimate.
5. How much hands-on time did preparation, execution, checking, and corrections take? Record waiting time separately. Whose time is included?
6. Which files and systems were needed? Who can grant access? Which records must remain restricted?
7. What tools or automations already help, who maintains them, and what still requires someone to intervene? Include informal workarounds and previous attempts.
8. What mistake would make the result unusable? Who catches it now, and who could review a small test?
9. If this job took less time, which specific work would you use the recovered hours for? If the goal is commercial, what customer action would show progress?
10. What would make you willing to try a change? What would make it harder to use during a busy release or reporting period?

## Interview follow-up

Ask the operator to show one permitted example. Then ask the recipient what they checked and what they sent back. Include a person who handles exceptions; an easy example alone will hide the expensive work. Preserve disagreement between roles until the team resolves it.

## Choose a measure

- Feasibility: accepted examples, failure types, and what remains unknown.
- Capacity: comparable tasks completed, total human time, corrections, and the work that receives recovered time.
- Commercial: qualified demand, paid outcomes, contribution after delivery costs, and a comparison that helps separate the test from other changes.

Hours recovered are capacity. Cash savings require a specific budget reduction or avoided expense that the budget owner confirms. Keep projected benefits separate from measured results.
