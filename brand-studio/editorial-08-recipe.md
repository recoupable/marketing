# Recoup editorial system — illustration and type

Proposed visual applications, September 11, 2026. This round replaces the photographic direction with the user's explicit preferences: predominantly one color, conceptual graphics, clear type, and no literal music imagery. Nothing in the set is approved for production yet.

## Reference patterns

References: https://claude.com/blog and https://www.langchain.com/blog

Recoup keeps its own blue, forest, lime, white, and pale-blue palette and open DM Sans voice. This study does not copy the references' marks, specific illustrations, or font identities.

## Fixed rules

- Aim for roughly 80% uninterrupted dominant color. A gradient is optional in future studies, but this controlled round uses flat fields.
- One illustration or abstract pattern per cover. Avoid photographs, realistic surfaces, musicians, instruments, music-note icons, documents, magnifiers, and checkmarks.
- Illustration uses thin, deliberate, slightly irregular lines and one or two solid shapes. Pattern uses precise curves, dots, or repeated lines with clear spacing.
- The graphic supports the title; it does not need to literally depict the industry.
- Type uses sentence case, DM Sans around weight 450, close natural tracking, and generous margins. Avoid heavy, condensed, outlined, shadowed, or novelty lettering.
- Keep logos out of this comparison. If added later, use one exact existing Recoup asset, never a generated interpretation.

## Prompt A — small illustration

Create one 16:9 Recoup editorial cover for [ARTICLE TITLE].

Dominant background: [ONE RECOUP COLOR]. At least roughly 80% of the image remains a quiet uninterrupted flat color field.

Visual idea: [ONE SIMPLE CONCEPTUAL METAPHOR]. Make one compact original editorial ink illustration, about 25–30% of the canvas width, near the center with balanced negative space. Use thin deliberate slightly irregular [INK COLOR] lines and one or two [FILL COLOR] shapes. Keep the drawing confident and spare. It should feel authored rather than like an off-the-shelf pictogram.

No embedded text; the article title sits below in the website's actual DM Sans. No people in photographic scenes, literal industry objects, thick cartoon outlines, icons, gradients, grain overlays, photorealism, 3D, shadows, labels, logos, or watermarks. Use the chosen approved illustration reference for line character and scale while inventing a new subject. Do not copy another company's artwork.

## Prompt B — headline and abstract pattern

Create one 16:9 Recoup editorial cover for [ARTICLE TITLE].

Dominant background: [ONE RECOUP COLOR]. Roughly 80% or more remains uninterrupted flat color. Headline color: [HIGH-CONTRAST RECOUP COLOR].

Exact headline: [SHORT CLEAR HEADLINE WITH INTENTIONAL LINE BREAKS]. Use the same open regular-weight DM Sans-style sans serif across the series. Left-align within a 7% safe margin, with generous space beneath. Keep one consistent size and hierarchy rather than filling the available area with oversized words.

Secondary graphic: [ONE ORIGINAL PATTERN OF CURVES, DOTS, OR REPEATED LINES], confined to a lower or outer portion of the frame. Precise spacing, thin strokes, purposeful cropping, and no overlap with the headline. Pattern should occupy about 20% or less of the composition. No decorative objects, pictograms, arrow diagrams, photography, 3D, gradients, shadows, texture, logos, watermarks, or extra words.

## Reuse

For each new article, vary the metaphor or pattern, dominant palette, and crop while preserving the chosen family's drawing and type system. Review against the last six covers. Do not randomize every design variable. Use factual screenshots or source material when the article requires evidence; an abstract cover is an editorial illustration, not research proof.
