# Recoup editorial use — thumbnails and article illustrations

September 11, 2026. The user selected the five round 08 type-and-pattern thumbnails. This study adds a restrained edge gradient and tests clearer illustrations inside sample article passages. The new assets remain proposed. The earlier abstract fragments and arch are not selected illustrations.

## The shared identity

Use Recoup blue, forest, lime, pale blue, and white. Keep one color dominant, generous negative space, thin deliberate lines, and a small number of accents. Actual text should use DM Sans around weight 450. No photoreal imagery, literal music-industry decoration, heavy outlines, novelty lettering, or unexplained abstract symbols.

## Thumbnail edit recipe

Use the selected original as the edit target. Preserve its exact headline wording, line breaks, type size, weight, placement, colors, margins, and pattern geometry. Add only a whisper of gradient at the outer edge behind the pattern: approximately 5% more atmosphere, with the rest visually unchanged. Keep the area behind the headline calm and the dominant field clearly recognizable. Use a soft smooth falloff with no hard boundary. Do not introduce grain, extra objects, logos, new text, strong neon blooms, or a broad blue-to-lime split.

Suggested pairings: blue with a faint cyan edge; forest with a quiet blue edge; pale blue with slightly deeper cyan; white with faint pale blue; lime with a trace of pale blue. Interpret 5% as restrained visual intensity, not a measured image-model opacity setting.

For new covers: vary the pattern geometry, crop, and dominant palette within this vocabulary. Preserve headline hierarchy. Review the last six covers to avoid repeating the same crop every time.

## In-article illustration recipe

Article passage: [THE PARAGRAPH THE IMAGE ACCOMPANIES]

One idea to make easier to understand: [SPECIFIC RELATIONSHIP OR STEP]

Draw [RECOGNIZABLE SUBJECTS] connected by [MEANINGFUL RELATIONSHIP]. Use thin deliberate editorial pen lines, restrained opaque fills, and the same Recoup palette as the thumbnails. The drawing should occupy enough space to be understood at article width. Label only the elements that need naming. Use the actual article's terms. Keep any numbers as source references or step labels, never invented performance statistics.

Supply a one-sentence caption that explains what the reader should notice. If the caption has to explain what an object is supposed to be, redesign the object. Avoid generic metaphors that cannot be understood without a title. Keep decoration secondary to the idea.

Choose the format according to the idea. Diagrams explain steps and relationships. Charts explain real data and comparisons; retain units, scale, and source attribution. Infographics organize related facts. Metaphorical or anthropomorphic illustrations are welcome when they make an idea more intuitive or memorable than a diagram. They can add personality and humor while keeping the subject and relationship understandable.

For process diagrams, labels and connectors must agree with the passage. For editorial interstitials, choose one immediately recognizable subject related to the nearby text. For metaphors and anthropomorphism, make the action or relationship easy to recognize; avoid symbols the reader must decode. Do not present illustrative content as evidence or a screenshot of a working product.

## Review gates

1. Can the headline be read first at thumbnail size?
2. Is the gradient still a small finishing detail?
3. Can a reader name the objects in the article illustration?
4. Does the image explain the same relationship as the accompanying paragraph?
5. Does the image add understanding beyond repeating the caption?

All new PNGs use the built-in image generation tool. Exact edit/generation prompts and source references are saved with each file. Generated text should be typeset as editable DM Sans when the final application is approved.
