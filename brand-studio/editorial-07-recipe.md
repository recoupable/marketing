# Recoup editorial imagery — round 07

These are proposed directions for visual review. They respond to the feedback that repeated outlined icons, loud gradients, and competing headlines made round 06 feel cheap. The earlier blue/lime preferences remain useful color references, not a requirement to reuse the same background.

## What connects the images

- Music and its business supply the subject: recordings, archives, instruments, performance, research, and the systems around them.
- Blue provides atmosphere; forest provides depth; ivory provides reading space. Lime can be a small deliberate accent, and may be absent.
- Light, material, crop, and negative space make the composition. Grain should belong to paper or a photographic process rather than coat every image.
- An article title sits beneath every image in the website's open, regular-weight sans serif. An integrated headline is an occasional editorial choice, not a required second title.

## Reusable prompt

Create one 16:9 editorial image for Recoup, for music executives, music funds, and rightsholders.

ARTICLE: [title]
ARGUMENT: [what the article actually explains]
VISUAL IDEA: [one specific subject, action, or relationship that connects to that argument]
MEDIUM: [one visually approved direction from this study]
COMPOSITION: [crop, point of view, focal point, and quiet space]
MATERIAL AND LIGHT: [a credible material and a deliberate lighting condition]

Keep the image connected to Recoup's website: open atmosphere, clear structure, tactile detail, spacious composition. Use a considered subset of blue, forest, ivory, and lime. Color can live in actual materials, daylight, shadows, or a photographic process. Avoid applying the same gradient to every subject. Preserve natural material and human colors.

Make one idea clear at thumbnail size. Use precise photographic or graphic construction appropriate to the medium. Avoid generic business clip art, decorative documents, magnifiers, checkmarks, cartoon outlines, meaningless sci-fi objects, and luxury clichés. A detailed realistic image still needs a clear subject and strong composition.

TYPE: [No text / exact short headline and a specified restrained typographic treatment]. Default to no text because the full article title appears below. If using a headline, reserve a calm area with generous margins, reproduce the words exactly, and keep it separate from essential subject detail. No invented logos or incidental lettering. Final production typography should remain editable outside the generated image.

RECENT SIX COVERS: [subject, medium, crop, dominant color, use of type]
Choose a relevant image, then vary at least two meaningful dimensions from nearby covers. Change perspective, subject scale, material, lighting, or medium rather than simply reversing a gradient. Deliberate series continuity is allowed.

Generate one full-bleed landscape image. Check subject, material, cropping, and any text before approval. When an article presents product behavior, research, historical evidence, or a real customer, use supplied factual assets rather than invented screenshots, figures, identities, or provenance.

## Visual review

Use Large artwork to assess craft, and Blog view to assess recognition and repetition. Select individual directions; ten different proposals do not need to become ten production styles. A small approved family of image approaches will make a stronger repeatable system.
