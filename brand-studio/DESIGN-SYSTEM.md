# Recoup design system — Edition 01

Recoup makes AI useful for the people who create, own, and invest in music. The identity should feel open, clear, capable, and human.

This document extends the current Sky marketing direction into a working brand system. Existing identity decisions are distinguished from proposed applications in the Studio’s review notebook.

## Identity and voice

**Our mission is to make AI work for the people who create, own, and invest in music. We help music companies put it to work across their business, so their teams can do more and their music can earn more.**

**The podcast is named The Recoup Podcast.** Episode titles can carry specificity and interest while the show name builds recognition for Recoup.

Personality should come through in the directness and confidence of the writing. It shouldn’t depend on making ordinary things sound more abstract.

| Avoid | Use |
| --- | --- |
| The context. Already there. | Investment review |
| Unlock your AI potential. | Find where AI can save your team time. |
| A film for your release. | A music video for your release. |
| Own every tool in your AI stack. | Working custom systems you own, connected to your tools. |

The subject must be clear from the headline. Let supporting copy add detail. Label services, process steps, and case studies accurately. Claims about results need evidence; example screens and templates are not client proof.

## Logo

Use the supplied symbol and outlined wordmark. Scale the complete mark proportionally. Do not redraw, stretch, reshape, or separate its internal geometry. The lockup preserves the website’s DM Sans 600 wordmark, tracking, and symbol-to-wordmark gap.

Use ink or blue on light surfaces; white or lime on forest. Keep the mark away from busy detail. As a starting layout rule, leave clear space equal to half the symbol’s height around the lockup. This clear-space rule is a proposed production convention, not a change to the logo.

| Sky | #007EBD | Large color surfaces and atmosphere |

| Pale blue | #F0F7FA | Gentle grouping and context |

White on Sky blue is about 4.45:1: suitable for large text, below the 4.5:1 target for normal text. Use Link blue for normal-size blue text on white. Ink on white, muted ink on white, and forest on lime meet normal-text contrast. Text over imagery needs a controlled surface or sufficient local contrast. Verify combinations in their actual context.

## Typography and hierarchy

- DM Sans Variable: headings 450–500, body 400, wordmark 600.
- IBM Plex Mono: short labels, metadata, code. Do not use it for long paragraphs.
- Begin body text around 16 px with 1.5–1.65 line height. Keep small metadata secondary and readable.
- Let the subject headline be the largest text. Labels inside an illustration should be visibly smaller.
- Use fluid headings, natural wrapping, and readable mobile padding. Avoid fixed heights that clip edited text.

## Three levels of expression

**Functional:** product, reports, documentation. Quiet surfaces, visible labels, accurate states, fast feedback. Information leads.

**Editorial:** articles, interviews, explainers. One subject, a considered composition, deliberate space. Show the work through papers, documents, connections, and physical materials.

**Expressive:** podcast, campaigns, launches. More scale, richer environments, and controlled motion. Faces and titles stay readable; the exact mark anchors the composition.

These share foundations. They do not require every product screen to look like a marketing hero.

## Visual world

The current sky and meadow establish openness, daylight, and natural texture. Proposed extensions include a sunlit listening room, restrained paper compositions, connected information, and simplified tools that show what work gets done.

Use soft daylight, subtle material texture, and a clear focal point. Leave deliberate space for headlines or video overlays. Keep decorative slogans out of illustrations. Prefer specific, understandable labels. Avoid a generic AI aesthetic of robots, neon circuitry, space scenes, and elaborate floating dashboards.

Generated images provide environments. Put logos and exact typesetting on top using editable templates rather than asking an image generator to recreate the brand mark.

## Components and motion

Use visible labels, clear keyboard focus, and honest success/error states. Primary actions use forest text on lime. Secondary actions have a quieter outline or text treatment. Announce saved states without moving the page unexpectedly.

Suggested motion: 180–240 ms for controls, 450–700 ms for short editorial reveals. Keep motion subordinate to reading. Honor reduced-motion preferences and preserve the content without animation. Logo intro/outro files are separate playback assets; they do not autoplay in the Studio.

## Templates and exports

The podcast creator provides thumbnails, square and portrait artwork, title cards, single and double speaker frames, a screen-share frame, and a transparent name label. Edit one episode and export the full set. Speaker windows must remain transparent when placed above video tracks.

Social templates include insight, quote, announcement, case study, carousel, and podcast clip canvases. Use the blank structure; replace example text with approved copy. Check platform cropping before posting.

## Reviews and versions

Each library asset has an ID, dimensions, preview, source files, and status. New assets start as proposed. Approval records should include the reason, reviewer, and date. Browser-local decisions are portable via JSON export; this first edition does not synchronize a team’s approvals.

Keep the recipe, chosen reference, generated source, final composition, and review together. Retire superseded versions rather than silently replacing the approved file.

## Titles tell the story

Headlines are optional: do not add one to every illustration or frame. Let a supporting figure
work through its visual and necessary short labels when the surrounding content provides context.
The narrative rule governs titles used together; it does not require a headline on every asset.

Across the Recoup brand, titles that work together must tell the full story when read alone in
sequence. This applies to article figures, carousels, slide decks, reports, web sections and video
title sequences. Draft the title sequence before designing individual frames. Each title advances
the argument; together they explain the premise, progression and conclusion without body copy or
images. Read only the titles in order during review. Rewrite any vague label, disconnected slogan
or missing transition until the reader can follow the complete narrative.

## Use the wordmark deliberately

Do not add a Recoup wordmark footer to every illustration or every frame in a series. Identify the
collection where useful, such as its enclosing page, cover or closing frame. Supporting figures
can stand without a mark. If a mark is used, preserve the exact supplied artwork. Generated
illustrations should contain no logo; apply any needed mark as a separate editable layer.

## Article covers earn attention

Treat the first article image as a thumbnail with its own composition. Use a bold, saturated Recoup
color field, a single high-contrast focal element and, when useful, a short oversized hook of 3–6
words. Make it recognizable and readable at about 160px wide. Supporting illustrations can remain
simple on white with short labels and optional headlines. Do not style the cover as another inline
figure. Color and scale should earn attention without misleading claims or extra body copy.
